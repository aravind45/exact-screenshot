### 5. Non-Probate Transfers
**Coverage: ❌ LIMITED**

These don't require court forms (by definition):

- **TOD/POD accounts**: Direct beneficiary designation
- **Joint tenancy**: Automatic transfer
- **Living trusts**: Trust administration (not probate)
- **Life insurance**: Beneficiary claim forms (from insurance company)
- **Retirement accounts**: Beneficiary claim forms (from custodian)

**Why no forms?**
- These bypass probate court entirely
- No judicial supervision required
- Forms come from financial institutions, not courts

**What you need instead:**
- Death certificate
- Claim forms from institutions
- Affidavit of domicile
- Small estate affidavit (if needed)

**Verdict**: ❌ Not applicable - these aren't court forms

---

### 6. Trust Administration
**Coverage: ❌ NONE**

Trust administration is separate from probate:

- No court involvement (usually)
- Trustee acts under trust document authority
- Different forms entirely (trust-specific)
- Only goes to court if contested

**Why no forms?**
- Trusts are private documents
- No standardized court forms
- Each trust has its own procedures

**What you need instead:**
- Trust document
- Trustee acceptance
- Notice to beneficiaries (state-specific)
- Accounting to beneficiaries
- Distribution receipts

**Verdict**: ❌ Different legal process - not probate forms

## 🔍 Unique State-Specific Forms Explained

### Texas: Independent Administration (TX-3)
**What it is**: Texas allows executors to act without court supervision
**Why it matters**: Faster, cheaper, more flexible than dependent administration
**Coverage**: ✅ Included

### Texas: Muniment of Title (TX-12)
**What it is**: Probate the will without appointing an executor
**When used**: No debts, simple estates, just need to transfer title
**Coverage**: ✅ Included

### Florida: Homestead Property (FL-15)
**What it is**: Florida's constitutional protection for family home
**Why it matters**: Homestead can't be devised if spouse/minor children survive
**Coverage**: ✅ Included

### New York: Ancillary Probate (ET-3)
**What it is**: Probate for non-residents who owned NY property
**When used**: Decedent lived elsewhere but owned NY real estate
**Coverage**: ✅ Included

### California: Spousal Property Petition (DE-221)
**What it is**: Confirm community property passes to spouse without full probate
**Why it matters**: Avoids full probate for community property
**Coverage**: ✅ Included
