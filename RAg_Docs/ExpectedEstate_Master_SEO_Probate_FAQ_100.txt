EXPECTED ESTATE – MASTER PROBATE SEO FAQ (100 QUESTIONS)

CORE PROBATE AUTHORITY SECTION

1.  What is probate? Probate is the court-supervised legal process of
    validating a will, appointing a personal representative, settling
    debts, and distributing assets.

2.  What does probate court do? Probate court oversees estate
    administration, validates wills, resolves disputes, and authorizes
    distributions.

3.  Is probate always required? No. Probate is required only for assets
    solely owned without beneficiary designations or joint survivorship.

4.  How do you start probate? File a petition in the county probate
    court where the decedent lived.

5.  What documents are required to file probate? Death certificate,
    original will (if available), petition forms, and list of heirs.

6.  How long does probate take? Typically 6–18 months depending on
    complexity.

7.  What delays probate? Creditor claims, disputes, tax filings, real
    estate sales.

8.  How much does probate cost? Court fees, attorney fees, executor
    compensation, appraisals, publication costs.

9.  Who pays probate fees? The estate pays before beneficiary
    distributions.

10. Can probate be avoided? Yes through trusts, POD/TOD accounts, and
    joint ownership.

EXECUTOR & ADMINISTRATION (11–25)

11. What are executor duties? Secure assets, notify creditors, file
    taxes, distribute assets.

12. Can an executor be removed? Yes, by court petition for misconduct or
    incapacity.

13. Does an executor get paid? Yes, statutory or reasonable
    compensation.

14. Is executor personally liable? Yes, for breach of fiduciary duty.

15. What is fiduciary duty? Legal obligation to act in beneficiaries’
    best interests.

16. Can executor sell property? Yes, sometimes with court approval.

17. What is estate inventory? List of probate assets and values.

18. What is estate accounting? Detailed record of estate income and
    expenses.

19. Can beneficiaries request accounting? Yes.

20. When can executor distribute assets? After debts and taxes are
    resolved.

21. What if executor refuses to act? Court may appoint replacement.

22. What is probate bond? Insurance protecting estate from executor
    misconduct.

23. When is bond required? If will does not waive it or court orders it.

24. What is Letters Testamentary? Court document granting executor
    authority.

25. What is Letters of Administration? Authority granted when no will
    exists.

PROBATE WITHOUT A WILL (26–35)

26. What is intestate succession? State rules determining inheritance
    without a will.

27. Who inherits if no will? Spouse, children, parents, siblings based
    on statute.

28. Does spouse inherit everything? Depends on state and presence of
    children.

29. Do stepchildren inherit? Generally no unless legally adopted.

30. What if no heirs exist? Estate may escheat to the state.

31. Can intestate estates avoid probate? Only if assets are non-probate.

32. How is administrator chosen? Court appoints closest qualified heir.

33. Are intestate estates slower? Often similar timeline.

34. Do intestate estates cost more? Not necessarily.

35. Can heirs disagree? Yes, court resolves disputes.

ASSET TYPES & SPECIAL SCENARIOS (36–60)

36. What are probate assets? Solely owned assets without beneficiaries.

37. What are non-probate assets? Life insurance, retirement accounts,
    trust property.

38. Does real estate go through probate? If solely owned, yes.

39. What is ancillary probate? Secondary probate in another state.

40. Do bank accounts go through probate? If no beneficiary or joint
    owner.

41. What about digital assets? Included if solely owned.

42. What is RUFADAA? Law governing fiduciary access to digital assets.

43. What happens to business interests? Subject to operating agreement
    terms.

44. What if estate is insolvent? Debts paid in statutory priority.

45. What are creditor claim deadlines? State-defined period after notice
    publication.

46. Are funeral expenses paid first? High priority claim.

47. Are taxes owed after death? Final 1040 and possible estate return
    required.

48. Does every estate pay federal estate tax? Only estates exceeding
    exemption threshold.

49. What is estate income tax (1041)? Tax on post-death estate income.

50. What is step-up in basis? Asset tax basis adjusts to date-of-death
    value.

51. What is a small estate affidavit? Simplified collection process
    below state limit.

52. What qualifies as small estate? Estate under state-specific dollar
    threshold.

53. Is probate public record? Yes.

54. Can probate be sealed? Rarely, under special circumstances.

55. Can beneficiaries contest a will? Yes.

56. What are grounds for contest? Undue influence, lack of capacity,
    fraud.

57. Can probate reopen? Yes, for newly discovered assets.

58. What is partial distribution? Early distribution before final
    closing.

59. Can probate cross state lines? Yes via ancillary proceedings.

60. What if beneficiary dies during probate? Distribution passes under
    anti-lapse rules.

INTERNATIONAL & VISA ISSUES (76–85)

76. Can foreign heirs inherit? Yes.

77. Are foreign beneficiaries taxed? Possible withholding applies.

78. What if deceased was non-resident? Probate required if assets in
    U.S.

79. Can documents require apostille? Yes for foreign use.

80. What if H1B holder dies? Estate handled under U.S. probate law.

81. Do dependents affect probate? Not legally, but may affect
    administration logistics.

82. Can estate funds be wired overseas? Yes after compliance checks.

83. Are international estates slower? Often due to documentation delays.

84. Can foreign executors serve? Sometimes with court approval.

85. Do immigration timelines affect probate? No direct effect on estate
    validity.

TRUST VS PROBATE (86–100)

86. Does a trust avoid probate? Yes for funded assets.

87. What is a pour-over will? Will directing assets into trust.

88. Is trust administration faster? Often yes.

89. Is trust private? Yes, unlike probate.

90. Does trust reduce cost? Often reduces court costs.

91. Can trust be contested? Yes.

92. Do trusts require accounting? Yes to beneficiaries.

93. What if trust is unfunded? Assets may require probate.

94. Are revocable trusts taxable? Included in estate for tax purposes.

95. What is successor trustee? Person managing trust after death.

96. Do trusts eliminate court? Generally yes unless dispute.

97. Is probate required for trust assets? No.

98. Should everyone avoid probate? Not always; depends on planning
    goals.

99. Can probate be simplified? Yes under small estate procedures.

100. How can executors stay organized? Use structured estate workflow
     tools.
