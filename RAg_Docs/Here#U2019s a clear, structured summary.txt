# **Summary of the Page**

## **1. The Reader’s Question**
A person received their late mother’s jewelry from their father over many years before he died.  
They now want to sell the jewelry but have **no paperwork** and are unsure:
- What **cost basis** applies  
- Whether taxes are owed  
- Whether the basis is tied to the mother’s death or the father’s death

---

## **2. Key Tax Distinction: Gift vs. Inheritance**
The columnist explains that:
- The jewelry was **a gift from the father**, not an inheritance from the mother.  
- Because it was a **lifetime gift**, the usual “step‑up in basis” that applies to inherited assets **does not apply** here.

**Implication:**  
The cost basis is the **father’s original basis** (what he or the mother paid), not the value at either parent’s death.

---

## **3. Gift‑Tax and Estate‑Tax Rules**
- The federal estate & gift‑tax exemption for 2025 is **$13.99M per person**.  
- Annual gift‑tax exclusion is **$19,000 per recipient**.  
- Gifts above that require filing a gift‑tax return, but most people never owe tax because of the large lifetime exemption.

---

## **4. Capital Gains Tax on Collectibles**
Jewelry is considered a **collectible**, so:
- Long‑term capital gains on collectibles are taxed at **up to 28%**, higher than typical capital‑gains rates.  
- High earners may also owe the **3.8% net investment‑income tax**.

---

## **5. Establishing Cost Basis Without Paperwork**
Because the reader has no documentation, the IRS may look for:
- Old appraisals  
- Estate documents  
- Gift‑tax filings (Form 706, Schedule A, Form 8971)

If none exist, determining basis becomes difficult, and a professional appraisal may be needed.

---

## **6. Broader Lessons for Families**
The article warns that:
- Giving valuable assets (jewelry, real estate, collectibles) to children during life can create **tax complications**.  
- Inheriting assets at death often provides a **more favorable tax outcome** due to the step‑up in basis.

---

## **7. Advice to the Reader**
- Consult a **tax adviser** experienced in estate and gift‑tax issues.  
- Get the jewelry **professionally appraised** if no prior valuations exist.  
- Selling the jewelry is fine, but taxes may apply depending on the gain.
