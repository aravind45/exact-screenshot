# **Summary of the Page**

## **1. The Core Problem**
An 80‑year‑old man has died **without a will, funeral plan, or instructions**, leaving his cousin — his only known family — unsure of what responsibilities they now carry.

---

## **2. Dying Without a Will (Intestate)**
- When someone dies **intestate**, state law decides who inherits their assets.  
- Most states follow a hierarchy:
  - **Spouse and children first**  
  - If none, then **parents**, then **siblings**  
  - If none, then **extended family**, including **cousins**  
- In this scenario, cousins may legally inherit if no closer relatives exist.

---

## **3. What the Cousin May Need to Do**
If no one else steps forward, the cousin may need to:
- **Open a probate case**  
- Potentially be appointed **administrator of the estate**  
- Locate and manage:
  - Bank accounts  
  - Insurance policies  
  - Retirement funds  
  - Personal belongings  
  - Outstanding debts  

This can feel like a **detective job**, especially if the deceased kept poor records.

---

## **4. Why This Situation Is Common**
- Two‑thirds of Americans do **not** have a will.  
- Many also fail to make funeral arrangements.  
- This leaves surviving family members to handle legal, financial, and emotional burdens during an already difficult time.

---

The article emphasizes that:

# ✅ **Checklist: What You Need to Do When a Cousin Dies With No Will**

## **1. Confirm Immediate Needs**
- Contact the hospital, care facility, or authorities to obtain:
  - **Death certificate** (order multiple copies)
  - Personal effects
- Arrange for the **body to be transported** to a funeral home.

---

## **2. Handle Funeral & Disposition**
Since there are no instructions:
- Choose **burial or cremation**.
- Select a funeral home and authorize services.
- Pay for arrangements (the estate reimburses you later if assets exist).

---

## **3. Secure Their Home & Belongings**
- Change locks if needed.
- Safeguard:
  - Mail  
  - Keys  
  - Important documents  
  - Valuables  
- Prevent utilities from being shut off prematurely.

---

## **4. Identify Whether You Are the Legal Next of Kin**
Because there is **no spouse, no children, and no closer relatives**, cousins may inherit under intestacy laws.  
You may need to:
- Confirm no closer relatives exist.
- Be prepared to act as the **estate administrator**.

---

## **5. Open Probate (If Necessary)**
If the cousin had any assets:
- File a petition with the local probate court to be appointed **administrator**.
- Provide the death certificate and basic family information.
- Notify creditors and heirs as required by state law.

---

## **6. Locate Assets & Accounts**
Search for:
- Bank accounts  
- Retirement accounts  
- Insurance policies  
- Real estate deeds  
- Vehicles  
- Safe‑deposit boxes  
- Digital accounts  
- Unclaimed property databases

This part often feels like detective work.

---

## **7. Identify Debts & Final Bills**
Gather:
- Medical bills  
- Credit card statements  
- Utility bills  
- Loan documents  
- Final rent or mortgage obligations  

The estate—not you personally—pays these.

---

## **8. Manage the Estate**
As administrator, you may need to:
- Inventory all assets  
- Maintain property (insurance, utilities, lawn care)  
- Sell items if needed to pay debts  
- File final tax returns (if required)  
- Keep detailed records for the court  

---

## **9. Distribute Remaining Assets**
After debts and expenses:
- Distribute what’s left to legal heirs under state intestacy law.
- As a cousin, you may be one of those heirs if no closer relatives exist.

---

## **10. Close the Estate**
- Submit final accounting to the court.
- Receive approval to close probate.
- Keep copies of all documents for your records.

#### Step 1: First 1–3 days — Immediate logistics
- **Confirm death and location:** Speak with the hospital, care facility, or authorities.
- **Choose a funeral home:** Arrange transport of the body.
- **Order death certificates:** Ask for multiple certified copies (bank, court, insurance, etc.).

#### Step 2: Days 3–7 — Funeral and basic security
- **Decide on disposition:** Burial or cremation, and any service you feel is appropriate.
- **Sign necessary authorizations:** With the funeral home or crematorium.
- **Secure their life:**  
  - Collect keys, wallet, phone, mail.  
  - Lock the home, close windows, and check pets (if any).  

#### Step 3: Week 1–2 — Information gathering
- **Look for documents:**  
  - ID, Social Security card, insurance, bank statements, pension, lease/deed, car title.  
  - Any old will (even if you think there isn’t one).
- **Make a simple family tree:** Confirm there’s no spouse, children, or closer relatives.
- **Start a folder:** Keep all bills, letters, and notes in one place.

#### Step 4: Week 2–4 — Decide if probate is needed
- **Assess assets:**  
  - Did they own a home, car, or have savings/retirement accounts?  
  - Or were they essentially without significant assets?
- **Call a local probate attorney (or court clerk):**  
  - Ask if you need to open probate.  
  - Ask what forms are required if you’re the one stepping up.
- **File to be administrator (if needed):**  
  - Petition the probate court to be appointed personal representative/administrator.

#### Step 5: Month 1–3 — Administering the estate
- **Open an estate bank account:** If the court appoints you.
- **Redirect and review mail:** Spot bills, statements, and possible assets.
- **Inventory assets and debts:**  
  - List accounts, property, and valuables.  
  - List medical bills, credit cards, loans, rent, utilities.
- **Pay necessary ongoing expenses:**  
  - Keep insurance and basic utilities on for any property.  
  - Do not pay debts from your own money—only from estate funds.

#### Step 6: Month 3–6 — Resolving finances
- **Settle debts in order of priority:** As required by your state’s law.
- **Close accounts:**  
  - Bank, credit cards, subscriptions, phone, internet.  
  - Notify Social Security and any pension provider of the death.
- **File final tax return (if required):** With help from a tax preparer if needed.

#### Step 7: Month 6–12 — Distribute and close
- **Distribute remaining assets:**  
  - Follow your state’s intestacy rules—cousins inherit only if no closer relatives exist.
- **Prepare a simple accounting:** Income, expenses, and distributions.
- **Submit final paperwork to court:** Request permission to close the estate.
- **Keep a final file:** Court orders, tax filings, closing letters, and a summary of what you did.
