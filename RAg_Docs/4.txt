# **Summary of the Page**

## **1. The Scenario**
A woman named **Becka** inherited her mother’s IRA.  
She must take a **$10,000 required minimum distribution (RMD)** in **2025**, but the account holds **no cash**—only stocks, bonds, and mutual funds.

---

## **2. Why This Is Complicated**
Inherited IRAs have special rules:
- The **SECURE Act 1.0 and 2.0** changed how non‑spouse beneficiaries must withdraw money.  
- If the original owner died in **2020 or later**, most adult children must **empty the account within 10 years**.
- If the original owner was already taking RMDs, the beneficiary must also take **annual RMDs**.

---

## **3. How the 10‑Year Rule Works**
- The 10‑year countdown starts **Dec. 31 of the year after the original owner’s death**.  
- Example from the article:  
  If Becka’s mom died in **2022**, the clock starts **2023**, and the account must be empty by **Dec. 31, 2032**.

---

## **4. What to Do When There’s No Cash in the IRA**
To take an RMD, Becka must **liquidate some investments** inside the IRA.  
Key points:
- She can sell **any combination** of stocks, bonds, or mutual funds to generate the needed cash.  
- The choice depends on:
  - Investment performance  
  - Risk tolerance  
  - Diversification  
  - Market conditions  
- Selling inside the IRA does **not** trigger capital gains taxes; the RMD itself is taxable income.

---

## **5. Why Professional Advice Helps**
The article strongly recommends consulting:
- A **financial advisor**  
- A **tax professional**  

Inherited IRAs are complex, and mistakes can lead to penalties or unnecessary taxes.

### Step 1: Confirm your RMD amount and deadline  
- **Find the exact RMD number** for this year (e.g., the $10,000 required in 2025).  
- **Note the deadline**—usually December 31 for inherited IRAs (unless it’s your first year with special rules).

---

### Step 2: List all holdings inside the IRA  
- **Make a quick table** of each position:  
  - **Name:** Fund/stock/bond  
  - **Type:** Stock, bond, mutual fund, ETF, cash equivalents  
  - **Current value & % of account**  
  - **Risk level:** Conservative / moderate / aggressive  

This gives you a snapshot of what you’re actually choosing from.

---

### Step 3: Decide your target mix *after* the sale  
Before picking what to sell, decide what you want the portfolio to look like going forward:  
- **More conservative?** You might trim stocks and keep more bonds/cash.  
- **Comfortable with current risk?** You might sell proportionally across all holdings.  
- **Too concentrated in one position?** You might use the RMD to reduce that concentration.

Your RMD is a chance to **reshape the portfolio**, not just raise cash.

---

### Step 4: Rank what’s most logical to trim  
Use these lenses to prioritize what to sell:

- **Overweight positions:**  
  - **Sell first** from holdings that have grown too large as a % of the account.  
- **Highest risk holdings:**  
  - If you want less volatility, **trim the riskiest stocks/funds**.  
- **Redundant or low‑conviction funds:**  
  - If you own multiple funds doing the same thing, consider simplifying.  

Create a short list: **“Sell from A, then B, then C if needed.”**

---

### Step 5: Check for practical constraints  
Inside an IRA, you don’t worry about capital gains, but you still check:

- **Trading costs or spreads:** Avoid very illiquid or expensive‑to‑trade positions unless it’s intentional.  
- **Minimum holding amounts:** Some mutual funds require a minimum remaining balance—don’t accidentally drop below it.  
- **Diversification impact:** Make sure selling doesn’t leave you overly concentrated in one sector or asset class.

---

### Step 6: Execute sales to raise slightly more than the RMD  
- **Sell enough to raise a bit more than the RMD** (e.g., $10,200 instead of $10,000) to cover any small price moves or fees.  
- Keep the extra cash in the IRA—you can always leave it there or use it for next year’s RMD.

---

### Step 7: Take the RMD distribution in cash  
- Once the sales settle, **withdraw the required amount in cash** from the IRA.  
- Remember:  
  - The **sale inside the IRA is not taxable**.  
  - The **RMD distribution itself is taxable income** (unless it’s from a Roth or has basis).

---

### Step 8: Decide what to do with the withdrawn money  
If you don’t need the cash immediately:  
- **Invest it in a taxable brokerage account** in a way that fits your overall plan (often tax‑efficient stock/ETF holdings).  
- Or **hold some in savings** if you want more liquidity.

---

### Step 9: Document your choices  
- Note **what you sold, why, and how much** you withdrew.  
- Keep a simple record so next year’s RMD decisions are easier and more consistent.
