# **Summary of the Page**

## **1. The Homeowner’s Situation**
- A 69‑year‑old woman, Lisa, placed her **$1.1 million home** into an **irrevocable trust** years ago.  
- Her two sons are both **trustees and beneficiaries**.  
- She now wants to **sell the home and downsize**, but isn’t sure whether the trust restricts how the sale proceeds can be used.

---

## **2. What an Irrevocable Trust Is**
- When you transfer a home into an irrevocable trust, **the trust—not you—owns the property**.  
- The grantor (Lisa) gives up control; the trustees (her sons) make decisions.  
- The trust generally **cannot be changed or dissolved** unless all beneficiaries agree or a court approves it.

---

## **3. Why People Use Irrevocable Trusts**
Common reasons include:
- Asset protection  
- Estate‑planning efficiency  
- Avoiding probate  
- Potential tax advantages  

---

## **4. The Core Issue: Can She Sell the Home?**
- Because the home is owned by the **trust**, the **trustees** must approve the sale.  
- The trust document determines:
  - Whether the home can be sold  
  - How the proceeds must be handled  
  - Whether the money must be reinvested in another property or kept in the trust

---

## **5. Key Takeaway**
Selling a home inside an irrevocable trust **is possible**, but the homeowner cannot act alone.  
The **trust terms** and the **trustees’ authority** determine what happens to the sale proceeds — including whether she must buy a home of equal value or can downsize freely.

# **Checklist: What Lisa Should Review in the Trust Document**

## **1. Ownership & Authority**
- **Who has legal authority to sell the home?**  
  Confirm whether the trustees (her sons) must act jointly or if one can act alone.
- **Does the trust require beneficiary consent** for major decisions?

---

## **2. Powers of the Trustees**
Look for a section titled **“Trustee Powers”** or similar:
- Does it explicitly allow the trustees to **sell real estate**?  
- Are there restrictions on **timing**, **conditions**, or **purpose** of a sale?
- Does the trust require the trustees to **maintain or reinvest** trust assets in a specific way?

---

## **3. Rules About the Home**
- Is the home designated as a **specific trust asset** with special instructions?  
- Does the trust require:
  - Replacement with a **home of equal value**?  
  - That the grantor (Lisa) must continue living in a trust‑owned property?  
  - That proceeds remain inside the trust?

---

## **4. Distribution & Use of Proceeds**
- After a sale, must the money:
  - Stay in the trust?  
  - Be invested in certain types of assets?  
  - Be used only for Lisa’s benefit?  
  - Be preserved for the sons’ inheritance?

---

## **5. Grantor’s Rights (If Any)**
Irrevocable trusts usually limit the grantor’s control, but check for:
- **Reserved rights** (rare but possible)  
- Any clause allowing Lisa to:
  - Request distributions  
  - Request changes  
  - Influence investment decisions

---

## **6. Beneficiary Provisions**
- Are the sons entitled to **immediate benefits** from the trust?  
- Does selling the home affect their **future inheritance**?  
- Are there rules about **equal treatment** of beneficiaries?

---

## **7. Tax‑Related Clauses**
- Does the trust specify who pays **property taxes**, **capital gains**, or **maintenance costs**?  
- Are there instructions about **basis**, **step‑up**, or **tax reporting**?

---

## **8. Termination or Modification Rules**
- Under what conditions can the trust be **amended**, **terminated**, or **restructured**?  
- Does it allow modification with:
  - Unanimous beneficiary consent?  
  - A court petition?  
  - A trust protector?

---

## **9. Successor Trustee Provisions**
- Who steps in if one son cannot serve?  
- Does a successor trustee have the same powers regarding selling property?
